## Overview

Teke Teke (テケテケ) is a Japanese urban legend that emerged in the 1980s and reached peak popularity in the 1990s. The legend tells of an onryō — a vengeful ghost — formed when a young woman or schoolgirl fell from a railway platform and was cut in two by an oncoming train. In most retellings she dies because of inadequate response by station staff and bystanders. Now lacking the lower half of her body, she travels on her hands or elbows at extremely high speed, dragging her bisected torso along the ground and producing a scratching sound rendered onomatopoeically as 'teke teke teke teke'. Encountering her at night, especially in train stations, school grounds, or empty streets, allegedly leads to pursuit; if she catches a victim, she severs them at the waist with a scythe or similar implement, perpetuating the cycle. Teke Teke is closely related to the older urban legend of Kashima Reiko, a girl in Hokkaidō traditionally said to have lost her legs in a railway accident; both legends share a railway-trauma origin. A Japanese horror film, 'Teketeke', was released in 2009, with a sequel the same year. The legend belongs to a broader cluster of Japanese urban-legend ghosts (kuchisake-onna, Hanako-san, aka manto) that became cultural touchstones in late-20th-century Japan.

## Etymology

- **Origin**: Japanese onomatopoeia 'teke teke', the scratching or scuttling sound she is said to make as she pulls her body along the ground.
- **Meaning**: Approximately 'scratch-scratch' — naming the entity after the sound of her movement.
- **First use**: 1980s schoolyard urban legends in Japan; popularised in the 1990s in tabloids and television.

### Skeptical

**Modern urban legend** _(Japanese folklorists (e.g. Tsunemitsu Tōru))_

Folklorists treat Teke Teke as a typical late-20th-century Japanese urban legend rather than as a paranormal report; its structure follows the schoolyard urban-legend pattern (rumour, ritual avoidance, transmission as warning).
